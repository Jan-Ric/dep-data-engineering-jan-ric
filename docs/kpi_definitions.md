# KPI Definitions

These metrics summarize modeled inverter-hour data and saved analysis tables. They support descriptive review, not diagnosis of real equipment faults. Throughout the detector analyses, `pr_gap` means the median valid PR across all inverters at a timestamp minus that inverter's PR at the same timestamp. It is not `1 - PR`.

## Thresholds

The saved three-hour query evaluates rolling sets of three consecutive hourly timestamps. Each hour must have irradiance above 200 W/m² and a valid PR; the three-hour mean `pr_gap` must be at least 0.05. Severity is Watch for gaps from 0.05 up to but not including 0.10, Alert from 0.10 through 0.20, and Critical above 0.20. These are demonstration thresholds, not validated maintenance limits.

## Detection Result

The saved validation has 12/12 designed Watch, Alert, and Critical windows detected with matching target tiers. The three hard windows were missed as expected: two have a mean gap below 0.05, and the third has a qualifying-sized gap but only two hourly records, shorter than the required three-hour run. The 12/12 result checks deterministic scenarios only; it is not evidence of real-world accuracy.

## False-Alarm Check

The saved check reports zero flagged blocks on non-faulted inverters. Zero is expected here because all non-faulted inverters share the same weather and the same simulated PR; this confirms the rule does not misfire on identical inverters and says nothing about noisy real data.

## Audience

The primary audience is an O&M engineer, who can use the metrics to prioritize records for review and verify the fixed detector against its test windows. A plant supervisor can use the fleet summaries to understand the scale and severity mix of modeled flags. Neither audience should treat a flag as a confirmed fault without real telemetry and investigation.
